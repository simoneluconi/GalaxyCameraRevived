# The lens profiles

Adobe lens profiles for the zoom lens (4.1–86.1 mm, f/2.8–5.9) of the Galaxy Camera and Galaxy Camera 2, one per camera:

| Camera | File |
|---|---|
| Galaxy Camera **EK-GC100** | [`profiles/EK-GC100-lens-profile.lcp`](../profiles/EK-GC100-lens-profile.lcp) |
| Galaxy Camera 2 **EK-GC200** | [`profiles/EK-GC200-lens-profile.lcp`](../profiles/EK-GC200-lens-profile.lcp) |

Both are generated by `tools/gc100-lcp.py`. They are also in every release.

## What they correct

The camera's RAW frame is the sensor's whole image, before the camera's own processing. At the wide end it shows the lens as it really is: strong barrel distortion, dark corners where the image circle ends, and corner shading. The camera's JPEGs hide all of that: the image processor straightens and crops them.

The profiles do the same for RAW (DNG) files in Lightroom, Camera Raw and RawTherapee:

- **Distortion**: the barrel distortion at the wide end, per focal length. It is strongest at 4.1 mm and fades out by about 16.5 mm. From 16.5 mm on, the profile does practically nothing, as the camera does.
- **Vignetting**: an even brightening towards the corners (about +0.7 EV in the corners), the same at every focal length.
- **Not chromatic aberration**: the profiles have no CA model. Use Lightroom's "Remove Chromatic Aberration" checkbox, or RawTherapee's CA correction, separately.

The JPEGs from the camera are already corrected. **Don't apply the profile to them.** The profiles are marked as raw profiles, so Lightroom offers them for raw files only.

## How to install and use them

### Lightroom Classic, Lightroom (desktop), Camera Raw

1. Copy the `.lcp` of your camera (or both) to:
   - Windows: `%APPDATA%\Adobe\CameraRaw\LensProfiles\1.0\`
   - macOS: `~/Library/Application Support/Adobe/CameraRaw/LensProfiles/1.0/`
2. Restart the application.
3. **DNGs from GC Camera** already ask for it: their XMP turns on "Enable Profile Corrections" and sets a crop that matches the camera's JPEG framing. Both can be switched off; the pixels in the DNG are untouched.
4. **Other DNGs from these cameras**: Develop (or Camera Raw) → Lens Corrections → Profile → tick "Enable Profile Corrections", then pick Make "Samsung", the "Samsung 4.1-86.1mm f/2.8-5.9" lens and the profile of your camera ("Samsung EK-GC100" or "Samsung EK-GC200") if they are not picked automatically.
5. The Distortion and Vignetting sliders under the profile scale its effect (100 = as measured).

Lightroom matches each profile by the DNG's Make (`Samsung`) and Model (`EK-GC100` or `EK-GC200`), so with both installed it picks the right one by itself.

### RawTherapee

1. Open the DNG, go to the **Transform** tab, and open **Profiled Lens Correction**.
2. Choose **LCP file** and pick the `.lcp` of your camera.
3. Leave "Geometric distortion" and "Vignetting" on.
4. At the wide end, crop the dark corners by hand (the Crop tool). RawTherapee does not read the Lightroom crop stored in the DNG.

RawTherapee picks the right entry from the DNG's focal length.

### darktable and others

darktable uses lensfun, not Adobe profiles, so it cannot use these files.

## How they were made

1. **RAW + JPEG pairs** across the zoom range, shot on an EK-GC100. For each shot the camera saved its RAW frame and its own JPEG.
2. **Registration.** Each JPEG was matched to its RAW with SIFT feature points, fitting a radial model. At the centre the JPEG is 1:1 with the RAW, not rotated.
3. **The fit.** Adobe's camera model: a corrected point comes from the image at `r × (1 + k1·r² + k2·r⁴)`, with `r` normalised by the half diagonal of the camera JPEG's frame (2880 RAW pixels). The measured values:

   | Focal length | k1 | k2 |
   |---|---|---|
   | 4.1 mm | −0.269 | +0.117 |
   | 5.0 mm | −0.177 | +0.050 |
   | 6.2 mm | −0.090 | +0.008 |
   | 9.2 mm | −0.025 | +0.010 |
   | 16.5 mm and up | none | none |

   The same table is in `camera-app/gccore/src/lens.cpp`, which the app uses for the DNG crop.
4. **The EK-GC200** has the same lens and the same 16 MP sensor, so it uses the same values. Its RAW frame is taller, and its lens sits at its own position on the sensor: its optical centre was found from the symmetric black border of the image circle in a wide-end RAW (the same method finds the GC100's measured centre within a few pixels).
5. **Vignetting** was not measured. The value (`VignetteModelParam1 = −0.375`) was set by eye in Lightroom, on real photos.
6. **The files.** `tools/gc100-lcp.py` writes each profile in exactly the shape of Adobe's own `.lcp` files (CRLF, ASCII, attributes on `rdf:Description`). Lightroom refuses other valid XMP shapes, and refuses a whole profile if any entry is all zeros, so the 16.5 mm and 86.1 mm entries carry a negligible k1 of −0.0001.

Each profile is defined for its camera's DNG default crop (8 px inside the RAW active area):

| Camera | Default crop | Optical centre in the active area |
|---|---|---|
| EK-GC100 | 4654 x 3436 px | (2331, 1739) |
| EK-GC200 | 4648 x 3478 px | (2321, 1694) |

To regenerate them: `python3 tools/gc100-lcp.py profiles/EK-GC100-lens-profile.lcp --camera EK-GC100`, and the same with `EK-GC200`.

## Limits

- **They reproduce the camera's correction, not a lab calibration.** The reference was the camera's own JPEGs, so whatever distortion the camera leaves in its JPEGs, the profiles leave too. From 16.5 mm on the camera corrects nothing and neither do the profiles.
- **Not every zoom step was measured.** Lightroom interpolates between the measured entries.
- **Corners at the wide end.** k2, which dominates in the far corners, is the least certain value.
- **Vignetting is set by eye**, a single value for all focal lengths and apertures.
- **One focus distance.** All entries are for focus at infinity; close-up shots may show small differences.
- **One unit per model.** The centre comes from one camera of each model; another unit's lens may sit a few pixels off.
